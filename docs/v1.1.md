# COP31 Monitor V1.1: human intelligence ingestion

Automated monitoring provides breadth. Human intelligence provides depth. Editorial verification builds trust.

V1.1 lets an editor turn a trusted contribution (a press-conference readout, expert analysis, meeting notes, a field report) into one or more **Today cards** and, optionally, a **full readout**. Everything unpublished stays outside the repository; publication still goes through `tools/today.py` and a named human approver.

## Design in one picture

```
DOCX / PDF / MD / pasted text
  │ tools/intake.py add            private copy + fingerprint, numbered paragraphs, contributor permissions
  ▼
private store (../cop31-private, or $COP31_PRIVATE; refuses to live in the repo or a clone of it)
  │ tools/intake.py prompt         extraction brief for the AI assistant (rules + ¶-numbered text + overlapping feed items)
  │ AI assistant → proposal.json   developments, claims with evidence category and verbatim quotes, optional article
  │ tools/intake.py propose        rejects anything that breaks provenance; writes draft cards + article draft + flags
  ▼
tools/today.py  show · set · link · resolve · i18n-export/import · publish · publish-article · correct · retract
  ▼
data/today.json, data/articles/<id>.json   (public: whitelisted fields only, validated in CI)
```

## Evidence categories (brief §3)

Every claim, every card field and every readout block carries one of four labels, shown on the site in all seven languages:

| Label | Meaning | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| verified fact | Confirmed by the editors against an official source | Needs an official https source; the editor marks it verified at `publish` |
| attributed | A statement by the person quoted, or the contributor's first-hand observation | Needs a speaker and a verbatim quote (≤ 40 words) from a numbered paragraph |
| analysis | The contributor's or editors' interpretation | Never allowed under "what happened" |
| unverified | An assertion not yet confirmed | Raises a flag that a named editor must resolve |

`propose` checks every quote against the extracted text and rejects a field that claims more than its weakest claim (an interpretation can never become a fact, and nothing becomes "verified" because of who said it). The AI cannot open web pages here, so it can only *propose* a verified fact; the editor confirms it.

## Two outputs from one contribution

- **Intelligence card** (`data/today.json`): headline, executive summary, what happened / changed / why it matters / what to watch, open questions, actors, evidence labels per field, contributor attribution, sources, cross-references to monitoring items, correction history. Mobile-first, same component as V1.0.
- **Full readout** (`data/articles/<id>.json`, route `#hoje/leitura/<id>`): the contributor's analysis in blocks, each labelled reported or analysis, in a minimal safe Markdown (paragraphs, lists, quotes, bold, italics, https links; raw HTML is escaped and rejected by the validator). The card links to it only once it is approved and published. JSON + Markdown keeps it static-site compatible: no CMS, no build step.

## Contributor governance

Recorded privately at intake: name, affiliation, role (author, observer, analyst, organization), confidentiality (public, chatham, confidential), permission to attribute by name (`named`, `anonymous`, `ask`), permission to publish the full analysis, permission to publish the document, and (for anonymity) an internal justification.

`publish` refuses when: the submission is confidential; the card names a contributor who has not authorized it; an anonymous card lacks a recorded justification or still mentions the contributor's name or affiliation anywhere; any flag is unresolved; translations are missing and not declared pending. A named affiliation always carries the line "the affiliation identifies the contributor and does not imply that the institution endorses the published interpretations". The original document is never published by these tools, even with permission (see limitations).

## Flags an editor must resolve before publication

`ai_draft` (always), `unverified_claim`, `verify_source`, `missing_source_url`, `chatham`, `confidential` (cannot be resolved into publication), `attribution` (preference not recorded), `possible_duplicate` (the importer found a monitoring item that may describe the same development; `today.py link` settles it, or the editor resolves it as distinct). Resolution is recorded with name, time and note; the audit trail stays in the private store (`published.json`).

## Deduplication and cross-referencing

V1.1 is manual by design: `today.py link CARD FEED_ITEM` records the cross-reference, and the card shows it as "In the monitoring". The importer suggests overlaps by term and figure overlap within a three-day window (`intake.py related`), raises a flag above a threshold, and never links automatically. Later: embeddings over feed items and published cards, clustering by development, and a "same development" relation shared across the feed, cards and readouts.

## Translation

`i18n-export` now covers the new fields (summary, open questions) and readout blocks; `i18n-import` records the method (`human`, `machine-reviewed`, `machine`). The site discloses machine translations and falls back to the Portuguese original, with a notice, for languages listed as pending. A correction to a published field removes that field's translations and lists the affected languages as pending until new translations are imported (the page says so instead of showing a stale translation). For diplomatic analysis, `machine-reviewed` or `human` is recommended.

## Corrections and retractions

`today.py correct` appends a public, dated correction note (and optionally replaces a field). `today.py retract` removes the card, removes its readout when no other published card uses it, and leaves a public retraction notice with the reason; the full record moves back to the private store.

## Security and confidentiality (brief §9)

- Private store outside the repository; `private.py` refuses a path inside the repository or inside any clone of the public repository; files are created 0600/0700.
- Public files are built from a **whitelist** of fields; the validator rejects any other field, any draft status, review markers, private-store paths, and raw HTML in readouts.
- `tools/check_public.py` runs in CI and as a pre-commit hook (`git config core.hooksPath tools/hooks`): it blocks tracked or staged `editorial/` files, original documents (.docx, .pdf, .doc, .odt, .rtf, .pages), private-store file names, and intake fixtures not visibly marked synthetic.
- `?preview=1` is not access control: drafts are simply never on the server. The page requests `__private__/` paths only on localhost, and only `tools/preview.py` (bound to 127.0.0.1) serves them.
- The legacy `editorial/drafts.json` is migrated into the store with `today.py migrate`; the tools warn while it exists.
- Screenshots of unpublished drafts go to the editor privately, never into the repository or the PR.

## Assumptions

- The AI step runs in the editor's Claude session (it reads `prompt.md`, writes `proposal.json`). No API keys live in the repository, and no tool calls a model or the web.
- Portuguese is the source language for cards and readouts, as for the rest of the site.
- Entry ids are derived from the event date, the submission title and the development key; readout ids from the submission id.

## Known limitations

- **No OCR.** Scanned PDFs are flagged; the editor asks for a text version.
- **The original document is never published** by these tools, even with permission: publishing third-party files needs a separate review (rights, metadata, tracked changes). If approved, attach it as a source URL hosted elsewhere.
- **Quote matching is literal** (typographic quotes, dashes, spaces and case are normalized). Paraphrased "quotes" are rejected by design; translated documents should be ingested in their original language.
- **Duplicate detection is a hint**, not semantic matching.
- **The private store lives on the editor's machine** (or this session's workspace). Back it up privately; a shared private repository is the next step (below).
- **Mobile rendering** is checked by CSS rules in tests and by screenshots; there is no automated visual regression.

## Future: Climate Intelligence Commons (documented, not implemented)

A federated contribution architecture for trusted networks, built as public-interest infrastructure:

1. **Contributors and institutions.** Each partner institution runs its own private intake (this tool, or a hosted equivalent) and holds its own contributors' identities and permissions. Institution-specific policies (who may attribute, embargoes, Chatham defaults) travel with each submission.
2. **Two layers.** A restricted layer (claims, evidence, drafts, shared among vetted editors under agreed rules) and a public layer (approved cards and readouts). Promotion from restricted to public is always an editorial act, recorded with approver and time.
3. **Open data standard.** Cards, readouts, claims and evidence categories published as versioned JSON (JSON Schema, later JSON-LD), with stable ids, so other trackers (think tanks, media, implementation organizations) can consume, cite and annotate without scraping.
4. **Open API.** Read-only endpoints for published content and feeds (static JSON first, then a small API), plus a submission API for authenticated partner instances; signed submissions (institution keys) carry provenance across organizations.
5. **Linked model.** Shared identifiers connect negotiation outcomes (decision paragraphs), policy changes (national measures), finance (pledges, flows) and implementation (projects), so a card about a roadmap can link to the decision text, the pledges and the delivery evidence.
6. **Independent editorial governance.** A charter (verification standards, corrections policy, conflict-of-interest rules), a rotating editorial board drawn from partners, and public correction logs. The platform amplifies existing think tanks, networks and media; it does not compete with them.
7. **Mobile contribution (first step).** Trusted experts submit by a private Drive folder or a dedicated address; an agent runs intake and drafting; the editor approves on the phone through a pull request that changes only `data/today.json` (GitHub's review and audit trail, no CMS or database).
